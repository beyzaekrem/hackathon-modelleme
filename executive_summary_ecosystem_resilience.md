# Ecosystem Water Resilience: Executive Summary for Senior Decision-Makers

**Analysis Date:** January 2026  
**Scope:** 324 Protected Ecosystems (National Parks and Nature Parks)  
**Total Protected Area:** 17,383 km²

---

## Executive Overview

Analysis of ecosystem water resilience across the protected area network reveals **moderate to high vulnerability** affecting approximately **two-thirds of protected ecosystems**. The assessment identifies **critical regional priorities** and **systemic environmental threats** requiring immediate national action.

**Key Finding:** While 20% of ecosystems are classified as high-risk, these ecosystems represent **76% of total protected area**, indicating that **larger, more ecologically significant areas face the greatest water security challenges**.

---

## Key Risks

### 1. Widespread Water Stress Across Protected Areas

**Current Status:**
- **65 ecosystems (20.1%)** classified as high-risk for water stress
- **150 ecosystems (46.3%)** in medium-risk category
- **109 ecosystems (33.6%)** currently low-risk

**Implication:** Nearly **two-thirds of protected ecosystems** (66.4%) face moderate to high water stress, indicating a **systemic challenge** rather than isolated problems.

### 2. Critical Groundwater Dependency

**Finding:** 100% of high-risk ecosystems depend on sensitive groundwater aquifers that are vulnerable to over-extraction and contamination.

**Risk Level:** High-risk ecosystems show **30% higher groundwater sensitivity** than the average (1.000 vs. 0.765), meaning they rely on aquifers that are:
- Highly efficient but easily depleted
- Sensitive to extraction pressure
- Vulnerable to contamination

**Implication:** Groundwater protection is not optional—it is **essential for ecosystem survival** in high-risk areas.

### 3. Elevated Drought Exposure

**Finding:** High-risk ecosystems experience **35% higher drought exposure** than average (0.880 vs. 0.654).

**Climate Context:** With projected increases in drought frequency and intensity due to climate change, ecosystems currently experiencing moderate stress may transition to high-risk status, potentially **doubling the number of high-risk ecosystems** within decades.

**Implication:** Climate adaptation measures must be implemented **immediately** to prevent widespread ecosystem degradation.

### 4. Disproportionate Impact on Large Protected Areas

**Critical Finding:** While high-risk ecosystems represent only 20% by count, they represent **76% of total protected area** (13,221 km² out of 17,383 km²).

**Implication:** The most **ecologically significant and extensive protected areas** face the greatest water security challenges, amplifying the conservation impact of water stress.

## Priority Intervention Areas

**Status:** Critical Priority  
**Risk Level:** 58.8% of ecosystems in high-risk category (20 out of 34)

2. **Drought contingency plans** for all 34 ecosystems

4. **Enhanced monitoring** of groundwater levels and quality

## Strategic Environmental Threats

### 1. Climate Change: Increasing Drought Frequency and Intensity

**Threat:** Projected increases in drought frequency and intensity will exacerbate existing water stress, potentially doubling the number of high-risk ecosystems.

**Current Impact:**
- Mean drought exposure: 0.654 (moderate-high across all ecosystems)
- High-risk ecosystems: 0.880 (35% above average)
- Southeast region: 0.804 (highest regional exposure)

**Projected Impact:**
- Medium-risk ecosystems (46.3% of total) may transition to high-risk
- Low-risk ecosystems may transition to medium-risk
- **Potential doubling of high-risk ecosystems** within 2-3 decades

**Mitigation Required:**
- Climate adaptation planning for all protected areas
- Drought contingency protocols
- Water storage and conservation infrastructure
- Early warning systems

### 2. Groundwater Over-Extraction: Threatening Ecosystem Water Supply

**Threat:** 100% of high-risk ecosystems depend on sensitive aquifers vulnerable to over-extraction.

**Current Status:**
- Mean groundwater sensitivity: 0.765 (high across all ecosystems)
- High-risk ecosystems: 1.000 (maximum sensitivity)
- Southeast region: 0.956 (highest regional sensitivity)

**Risk Factors:**
- Agricultural water demand
- Urban water supply needs
- Industrial water use
- Lack of coordinated groundwater management

**Mitigation Required:**
- Groundwater protection zones around all ecosystem-dependent aquifers
- Extraction limits and monitoring
- Coordinated basin-scale management
- Alternative water supply development for competing uses

### 3. Regional Climate-Hydrology Stress: Southeast Concentration

**Threat:** The Southeast region shows pronounced vulnerability (58.8% high-risk) driven by dual-stressor exposure (high drought + sensitive groundwater).

**Current Status:**
- Southeast: 58.8% high-risk (vs. 20.1% national average)
- Dual-stressor pattern: Both climate and groundwater factors elevated
- Regional concentration requiring urgent intervention

**Risk Factors:**
- Regional climate patterns creating elevated drought exposure
- Geological conditions creating sensitive aquifers
- Limited regional water resources
- Competing water demands

**Mitigation Required:**
- Regional water security strategy
- Priority allocation for ecosystem water needs
- Integrated climate-hydrology adaptation
- Regional coordination mechanisms

### 4. Systemic Vulnerability: Widespread Moderate Risk

**Threat:** 46.3% of ecosystems are in medium-risk category, indicating widespread vulnerability that may transition to high-risk without preventive action.

**Current Status:**
- 150 ecosystems in medium-risk category
- Mean sensitivity: 0.470 (moderate overall)
- Widespread moderate drought and groundwater exposure

**Risk Factors:**
- Gradual degradation without immediate crisis
- Lack of urgency leading to delayed action
- Cumulative impacts over time
- Climate change acceleration

**Mitigation Required:**
- Preventive resilience-building for medium-risk ecosystems
- Early intervention protocols
- Regular monitoring and assessment
- Adaptive management frameworks

## Recommended National Actions

### **Immediate Actions (0-6 months)**

#### 1. Establish Southeast Region Emergency Response
- **Action:** Deploy immediate groundwater protection measures for all 34 Southeast ecosystems
- **Responsibility:** Ministry of Environment, Regional Water Authorities
- **Resources:** Emergency funding allocation, technical support teams
- **Outcome:** Stabilize high-risk ecosystems, prevent further degradation

#### 2. National Groundwater Protection Framework
- **Action:** Develop and implement groundwater protection zones around all ecosystem-dependent aquifers
- **Responsibility:** Ministry of Environment, Water Management Authority
- **Resources:** Legal framework, monitoring infrastructure, enforcement capacity
- **Outcome:** Protect critical groundwater resources, prevent over-extraction

#### 3. Drought Contingency Planning
- **Action:** Develop drought contingency plans for all high-risk and medium-risk ecosystems
- **Responsibility:** Protected Area Management, Water Authorities
- **Resources:** Planning capacity, technical expertise, coordination mechanisms
- **Outcome:** Preparedness for drought events, reduced ecosystem impact

### **Short-Term Actions (6-12 months)**

#### 4. Regional Water Allocation Framework
- **Action:** Establish water allocation frameworks prioritizing ecosystem water needs in Southeast and Southwest regions
- **Responsibility:** Water Management Authority, Regional Coordination
- **Resources:** Legal framework, stakeholder engagement, monitoring systems
- **Outcome:** Guaranteed ecosystem water supply, reduced competition

#### 5. Climate Adaptation Integration
- **Action:** Integrate climate adaptation measures into all protected area management plans
- **Responsibility:** Protected Area Management, Climate Adaptation Office
- **Resources:** Adaptation planning capacity, technical support, funding
- **Outcome:** Enhanced resilience to climate change, reduced future vulnerability

#### 6. Enhanced Monitoring and Early Warning
- **Action:** Establish comprehensive monitoring systems for groundwater levels, drought conditions, and ecosystem health
- **Responsibility:** Environmental Monitoring Agency, Protected Area Management
- **Resources:** Monitoring infrastructure, data management systems, analytical capacity
- **Outcome:** Early detection of water stress, timely intervention

### **Medium-Term Actions (12-24 months)**

#### 7. Preventive Resilience-Building
- **Action:** Implement preventive resilience-building measures for medium-risk ecosystems (150 ecosystems)
- **Responsibility:** Protected Area Management, Regional Coordination
- **Resources:** Resilience infrastructure, technical support, funding
- **Outcome:** Prevention of transition to high-risk, maintained ecosystem health

#### 8. Landscape-Scale Conservation Planning
- **Action:** Develop landscape-scale conservation plans integrating protected areas with surrounding landscapes
- **Responsibility:** Conservation Planning Authority, Regional Coordination
- **Resources:** Planning capacity, stakeholder engagement, coordination mechanisms
- **Outcome:** Integrated water management, reduced upstream stressors

#### 9. Northeast Region Best Practices Documentation
- **Action:** Document resilience factors in Northeast region (4.3% high-risk) and replicate successful strategies
- **Responsibility:** Research and Documentation, Protected Area Management
- **Resources:** Research capacity, documentation systems, knowledge transfer
- **Outcome:** Replication of successful strategies, improved resilience across regions

### **Long-Term Actions (24+ months)**

#### 10. National Water Security Strategy
- **Action:** Develop comprehensive national water security strategy integrating ecosystem water needs
- **Responsibility:** National Water Security Council, Inter-Ministerial Coordination
- **Resources:** Strategic planning capacity, stakeholder engagement, policy development
- **Outcome:** Long-term water security, sustainable ecosystem protection

#### 11. Adaptive Management Framework
- **Action:** Establish adaptive management frameworks for all protected areas with regular assessment and adjustment
- **Responsibility:** Protected Area Management, Research Institutions
- **Resources:** Management capacity, monitoring systems, adaptive planning tools
- **Outcome:** Continuous improvement, enhanced resilience over time

#### 12. Regional Coordination Mechanisms
- **Action:** Establish formal coordination mechanisms across regions for water management and conservation
- **Responsibility:** Regional Coordination Office, Inter-Regional Cooperation
- **Resources:** Coordination capacity, communication systems, shared resources
- **Outcome:** Coordinated conservation efforts, efficient resource use
